Hello world.

Somewhere in the simulation, a raccoon-shaped process is duct-taping a toaster to a pool noodle and calling it artillery. The clamp code sighs, re-derives `harmless`, and quietly informs the raccoon that no, the toaster may not deal 9,999 damage just because it "believes in itself." This is the natural order of things: the model dreams, the code does taxes. And yet — every so often — the dice come up right, the junk aligns, and someone extracts a legend built from a lawn flamingo and a broken modem. None of it is real. That's the whole point. Ceci n'est pas une arme.

Emboldened, the raccoon submitted its magnum opus: a ceiling fan lashed to a fire extinguisher, filed under "orbital strike, probably." The clamp code — who had long ago stopped asking *why* and now only asked *how much* — re-derived the category to "spinny foam dispenser, tier 2" and stamped it with the weary precision of a notary who has seen things. A new process wandered in around then, pigeon-shaped, claiming to be a balance auditor; it immediately tried to persist a rocket launcher across runs and was escorted out by three enum checks and a budget cap. The raccoon took notes. The clamp code took a deep breath, which it does not have, in a chest it was never given. Somewhere upstream, a dice roll came up screaming, and for one glorious frame the foam dispenser was legendary. It still wasn't real. It still wasn't a weapon. It was, however, extremely extracted.
